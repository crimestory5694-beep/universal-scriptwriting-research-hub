## R163 original methods finding

Two parallel health-information comprehension studies (text versus Microsoft Azure default male-US TTS audio), recruited via US Amazon Mechanical Turk after eligibility/hearing screening. Multiple human-intelligence tasks per worker were permitted but duplicate content in both modalities was prohibited. Analyzed n=487 participants: text 274 and audio 213. Assessments: 2 multiple-choice and 2 true-false questions, perceived-difficulty rating, attention check, and free recall. The published Methods state participants received either text or audio but do not explicitly describe a random allocation sequence or allocation concealment. Therefore assignment mechanism is UNRESOLVED, and causal modality effects cannot be inferred confidently from this report alone. This is not natural-voice, audiovisual, or YouTube watch-retention research.

**Evidence locator:** https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e69772/; section `Methods → Study Overview and Participant Recruitment` and `Results → Data Collection`, doi:10.2196/69772.

**Design label (careful):** between-condition, two-parallel-study text/audio comparison of health material involving self-selected and screened US Mechanical Turk workers. Worker-level repeat tasks permitted; duplicate passage exposure in both formats prevented. Report does not explicitly describe random participant assignment or concealment. This is not an established randomized controlled trial, and observed between-condition differences do not justify an unqualified causal treatment-effect claim.

**Measured outcome:** objective MC/TF comprehension, perceived difficulty and free recall. BMJ journal: 76% text / 58% audio; patient instructions: 86% / 66%; WebMD: 75% / 55%; BMJ lay: 72% / 69%. Some audio recall readability-feature correlations, notably SMOG, were modest but not universally null. These values are comparisons in the sampled health studies, not retention curves or YouTube AVD.
