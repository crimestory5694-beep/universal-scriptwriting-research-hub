# 05 — Frameworks and Workflows

Models shown here are taken from reviewed sources. We did not create replacement acronyms or endorse causal outcomes.

## Five story-hook options
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0013 — 5 Best Ways to Start Your Story - Storytelling Hooks
- **Components / ordering:** Opening in action → crucial dialogue → relatable question → character snapshot → pivotal cliffhanger
- **Nature / dependencies:** **Important:** the five are alternatives, **not** ordered steps that must all appear.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Short narrative or personal anecdote; creator reports attention benefit, no retention A/B test.

## Five-step journey/story formula
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0123 — The Storytelling Formula YouTubers Use to Hook You
- **Components / ordering:** 1. Hook with goal/problem (plus real preview of obstacles). 2. Context: why, trigger, plan. 3. Rising action: alternating highs/lows and escalating genuine obstacles. 4. Climax: reveal decisive outcome and change. 5. Reflection: relatable lesson.
- **Nature / dependencies:** This is an explicitly sequenced five-phase model.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Goal-driven creator challenges; documentary narration needs truthful time/order.

## Progressive five-technique revision
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0018 — 5 Storytelling Techniques to Tell Insanely Good Stories
- **Components / ordering:** Baseline student story → raise questions → stakes → outer dialogue → inner dialogue → embodied emotion.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Creator revisits same hypothetical student story with each added element; examples are separate demonstration variants, not an empirically tested sequence.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Fictional study/education illustration.

## Seven storytelling techniques
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0021 — 7 Storytelling Techniques to Tell Great Stories
- **Components / ordering:** One-line advance promise; goals; obstacles; physical emotion; raw thoughts; a real deadline; crucial dialogue.
- **Nature / dependencies:** The 7 numbered tips are a toolkit, not necessarily a rigid workflow.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Casual spoken stories, personal video.

## Five mistakes and fixes
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0017 — 5 Storytelling Mistakes That Ruin Your Stories
- **Components / ordering:** Slow start → enter challenge; indifference → stakes; abstract report → zoom into scene; stiff voice → conversational; preachy ending → let story carry lesson.
- **Nature / dependencies:** A negative-advice checklist, not a creator-named mechanistic psychological model.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Short story to friends/coworkers/viewers; business ending has a stated exception.

## Hook, content, CTA video plan
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0150 — How I Write YouTube Video Scripts — Nolan Molt / Think Media
- **Components / ordering:** Opening benefit/promise → prepared explanation → related next-video CTA.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Creator calls it generic/simple; his separate writing workflow is hook verbatim + content and CTA bullets for confident speaker.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Presenter-driven instructional YouTube.

## Full-script versus bullet-outline decision
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0150 — How I Write YouTube Video Scripts — Nolan Molt / Think Media
- **Components / ordering:** Complete word-for-word script if needed for memorization/teleprompter; notes for easy on-camera improvisation. Gradually practice converting one or two ideas to bullet form.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Alternative routes and practice progression, not an absolute requirement that every filmmaker switch.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Talking head; sensitive evidence-driven voiceover may need fixed text.

## ERCRT (spelling preserved)
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0114 — Storytelling Science To Hook Viewer Till End  4 Years in 10 Mins.
- **Components / ordering:** E—Emotion cycle; R—Relatability; C—Climax at start (preview); R—Reason of watching; T—Twisting patterns.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Named formula creator explicitly teaches; psychological and retention outcomes not externally tested.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Creator-led storytelling and visual emotion pacing; actual editorial performance unknown.

## WHW
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0114 — Storytelling Science To Hook Viewer Till End  4 Years in 10 Mins.
- **Components / ordering:** What, How, Why as the creator says; the speaker also explains Why, How and What in prose.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Do not silently force canonical order not consistently demonstrated.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Brand and educational stories; adaptation scope unverified.

## TAM and Lens positioning
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0149 — Hindi teaching — YouTube niche, TAM, lens and AVE
- **Components / ordering:** 1. Estimate addressable audience/market using defensible data, 2. Define viewing lens (for whom, why, how).
- **Nature / dependencies:** TAM dollar examples in transcript are illustrative, ASR-garbled, and not validated financial forecasts.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Channel topic/audience decisions, not narrative script design.

## AVE — avatar, value loop, expansion
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0149 — Hindi teaching — YouTube niche, TAM, lens and AVE
- **Components / ordering:** A—Select avatar/audience before topic. V—Repeat expected audience benefit/engagement signal. E—Expand to related subjects for same people.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Creator-named audience-development framework; algorithm mechanism remains author theory.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Channel development/ideation; do not misstate platform recommendation system.

## Screenplay scene element sequence
- **Source:** EDU-SRC0148 — Film Script Formatting — Michaela Lizac
- **Components / ordering:** Slugline (INT/EXT and location/time) → visual action (capitalize initial character appearance) → dialogue with speaker cues and optional brief parentheticals → transitions if needed.
- **Nature / dependencies:** Production script formatting, **not** an automatic template for an audio-only YouTube documentary.
- **Applicability and evidence:** Film/TV; verify local style guide for cue definitions.

## Important non-equivalences

- The five options for *opening* a story are not the same as the five *stages* of a complete YouTube journey.
- ERCRT is a creator-named emotional/attention framework; it is not a scientific neural model.
- AVE concerns channel audience targeting, not word-for-word hook construction.
- Screenplay INT/EXT and parentheticals belong to film production; a faceless narrated video may instead use a separate, format-specific scene plan.
