# 08 — Cross-Creator Comparison

Cross-source repetition is **not** empirical replication. Most archive items have no confirmed creator/channel names, so even independence of nominally different videos cannot always be established.

## Repeated ideas and their differences

### Comparison 1: Consequence before lengthy exposition
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0123, EDU-SRC0017, EDU-SRC0021, EDU-SRC0018
- **Convergence:** Creators repeatedly say an explicit goal and its consequences can make early action meaningful.
- **Difference/contradiction:** One source says 2–3 sentences of context for short personal anecdotes; the five-stage marathon model adds motivation and a rough plan after a hook.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Plausibly transferable to human-interest documentaries; do not suppress essential factual setup.

### Comparison 2: Cliffhanger/question versus promise
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0013, EDU-SRC0018, EDU-SRC0123, EDU-SRC0150
- **Convergence:** Creators recommend a meaningful unresolved question or an initial promise.
- **Difference/contradiction:** For educational presenter videos Nolan favors a concrete learning benefit; character stories feature uncertainty about success.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Applicable only if withheld info is not misleading and payoff is delivered.

### Comparison 3: Concrete scenes versus summary
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0017, EDU-SRC0021, EDU-SRC0018, EDU-SRC0148
- **Convergence:** Concrete action, seen gestures, observed interaction are preferred to vague declarations in story settings.
- **Difference/contradiction:** The screenplay source gives production layout and emphasis conventions absent from casual verbal tales.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Film formatting itself is context-specific and not transferrable as general voiceover structure.

### Comparison 4: Interior emotion
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0021, EDU-SRC0018, EDU-SRC0017
- **Convergence:** Inner thoughts and bodily expression recur as suggested ways to convey emotion.
- **Difference/contradiction:** Personal recalled experience differs from third-person biography; factual minds must not be fabricated.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Historical/aviation incident narrations require sourced quotations/testimony.

### Comparison 5: Tension by obstacles
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0123, EDU-SRC0021, EDU-SRC0018
- **Convergence:** Obstacles, stakes and time constraints are recurring tools.
- **Difference/contradiction:** Five-stage narration favors escalating real obstacles; seven techniques lists deadline as one optional tool.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** In news, do not invent urgency or rewrite chronology.

### Comparison 6: Narrative takeaway
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0017, EDU-SRC0123
- **Convergence:** Both treat ending meaning as important.
- **Difference/contradiction:** **Tension:** one advises avoiding a preachy stated moral, while another ends with one explicit reflection relevant to viewer.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Not necessarily contradictory: reflection can be concise, first-person and non-preachy; test based on audience and genre.

### Comparison 7: Word-for-word versus bullet notes
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0150, EDU-SRC0148
- **Convergence:** Different formats use different degree of manuscript formality.
- **Difference/contradiction:** Nolan uses scripted hook and mostly bullet-point on-camera tutorial; Michaela teaches fully formatted screenplay dialogue.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** No universal production rule follows from this contrast.

### Comparison 8: Feeling and audience alignment
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0114, EDU-SRC0149, EDU-SRC0123
- **Convergence:** A narrative may have emotional change and a specific implied audience.
- **Difference/contradiction:** The Hindi niche tutorial concerns channel audience identity; ERCRT concerns moment-to-moment storytelling; these solve different problems.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** No algorithm benefit can be inferred.

### Comparison 9: Mid-video teaser and CTA
- **Source IDs:** EDU-SRC0150, EDU-SRC0123
- **Convergence:** Both creator tutorials use related-next-video promotion; Nolan additionally recommends a mid-video interaction request.
- **Difference/contradiction:** Neither confirms measured retention or algorithm effects. Mid-rehook and mid-CTA must not be conflated.
- **Interpretation and conditions:** Channel policy may prohibit a mid-CTA while retaining a narrative rehook.

## Single-creator or poorly replicated teachings

- ERCRT and WHW: EDU-SRC0114 only in reviewed sample. An apparent branded model is not a scientific test.
- AVE, TAM and Lens: EDU-SRC0149 only in reviewed sample, focused on channel positioning.
- INT/EXT, OC/OS, actor parentheticals: EDU-SRC0148, screenplay-specific and not evidence about viewer retention.
- Numeric 60–75% rehook / mid-CTA placements: EDU-SRC0150 alone in sample, internally given as an approximate range.

## Comparative constraints

- Only nine source texts were deeply reviewed in this batch. The remaining 153 may add repetitions, caveats, counterexamples or genuinely new models.
- Video creators are frequently not reliably identified. Shared channels, copied demonstrations, and influencer cross-pollination cannot yet be ruled out.
- Frequency across sources must not be translated to scientific confidence, retention lift, approval for production, or causal proof.
- No original video visuals or audio were inspected. Any production-based comparison refers to what the transcript says, not observed media.
