# 10 — Roman Urdu Research Brief (Batch 01)

## Sab se pehle scope samjhein

Aap ke 12 ZIP archives mein 266 TXT entries thin. Exact duplicate comparison ke baad 137 unique archive contents mile; 129 entries duplicated copies thin. Jo 4 alag TXT/Markdown uploads hain unmein multiple videos aur aik written skills resource bhi hai. In sab ko mila kar **162 alag source units** inventory mein hain.

Is batch mein **9 sources ka detailed teaching extraction** kiya gaya hai aur **109 provisional claims** banayi gayi hain. Baqi **153 source units sirf inventoried** hain; unko padh kar rules nikaalne ka kaam abhi baqi hai. Isay poore corpus ki extraction samajhna ghalat hoga.

## 1. Opening hooks ke sources kya sikhate hain

Aik video paanch opening options deti hai: (a) seedha action se shuru karein, (b) koi important conversation ka jumla pehle laayein, (c) relatable sawaal karein, (d) kisi character ki unique baat dikhaayein, (e) sab se critical lamhay ka hissa dikha kar us ki tafseel baad mein samjhayein. Ye paanch **alternatives** hain—har video mein sab lagana zaroori nahi. Source EDU-SRC0013; EDU-C0001–EDU-C0006.

Doosri source batati hai ke hook mein protagonist ka goal bata dena useful ho sakta hai: viewer ko sawaal milta hai ke kya woh goal hasil hoga? Agar video educational hai to Nolan Molt ka tareeqa mukhtalif hai: viewer ko pehle batayein ke woh kya seekhega. Yeh do formats alag hain; personal challenge ki technique ko har technical explainer mein zabardasti nahi daalna. Sources EDU-SRC0123, EDU-SRC0150.

## 2. Story ko beech mein interesting kaisay rakhein

Paanch-step story model: **Hook → Context → Rising Action → Climax → Reflection**. Goal pehle; phir bataayein goal kyun important tha aur plan kya tha; phir asli mushkilaat, progress aur setbacks; akhir mein decisive result aur relevant reflection. Yahan **asli sources ke mutabiq** tension dikhani chahiye, ghair-haqiqi drama create nahi karna. Source EDU-SRC0123.

Saat-technique wali teaching ke mutabiq: goal, genuine obstacles, visible emotional expressions, character ke thoughts, real time pressure aur decisive dialogue se story zyada scene-like ban sakti hai. Magar agar aap historical ya news script bana rahe hain, kisi soldier, scientist ya official ke andar ke thoughts ya exact baat **bina evidence invent nahi kar sakte**. Source EDU-SRC0021.

## 3. Before/after examples se kya samajh aata hai

Aik teacher ne simple fictional example diya: student David exam ki tayari karta hai, phone distractions hoti hain, friend app suggest karti hai, akhir mein usay acha grade milta hai. Creator isi same baseline mein alag alag cheez add karta hai—curiosity, stakes, outer dialogue, inner dialogue, embodied emotion. Is tareeqay se **technique samajhna** asaan hota hai. Lekin yeh story khud experiment nahin hai; retention data nahi diya gaya. Source EDU-SRC0018.

Aik aur creator kehta hai ke agar kahani der tak weather/background mein phansi rahe, ya stakes na hon, ya sirf broad summary ho, audience interest kho sakti hai. Uska remedy short personal anecdote mein challenge ke qareeb entry, chhota zaroori context, aur exact observable moment hai. Woh artificial storytelling voice aur preaching ending se bhi mana karta hai. **Exception:** business story mein learning ko seedhay lekin natural alfaaz mein keh sakte hain. Source EDU-SRC0017.

## 4. Script writing versus speaking ka real farq

Nolan Molt (Think Media) educational talking-head videos ke liye opening hook word-for-word likhta hai, jabke main body aur end mein bullet points istemal karta hai. Woh batata hai ke beginner full script se bhi kaam shuru kar sakta hai; phir chhoti chhoti sections ko bullet form mein laa kar practice kare. Isko faceless documentary narration ka mandatory rule **na** samjhein. Woh mid-video like/comment CTA aur baad mein rehook ki recommendation bhi deta hai—yeh creator ki recommendation hai, platform-proven rule nahi. Source EDU-SRC0150.

Michaela Lizac doosre format, yani **film screenplay**, ki terminology sikhati hain: INT/EXT slugline, scene action, dialogue cue, parenthetical, aur cut/transition. Inhein YouTube narration file mein automatic apply nahi karna. Yeh filmmaking formatting hai; VO narration ki storytelling quality ka direct saboot nahi. Source EDU-SRC0148.

## 5. Hindi transcript se audience/niche ki learning

Hindi creator ka central message yeh hai ke 'niche' ko sirf broad topic na samjhein—pehle yeh identify karein ke content kis aadmi ke liye, kis wajah se, aur kis point of view se ban raha hai. Isay **Lens** kehta hai. Woh total addressable market (TAM) aur audience size ka concept bhi discuss karta hai. **AVE** ke teen steps: Avatar (target person), Value Loop (woh value jo baar baar usay chahiye), Expansion (usi person se related naye topics). Source EDU-SRC0149.

Is creator ne recommendation algorithm ko example se samjhane ke liye 20 logon ka initial sample aur 50–70% reaction ka zikr kiya. Yeh verified YouTube threshold nahi hai. Isi tarah graphic designer income/TAM ke numbers ko real business forecast mat samjhein; kuch raqam transcript mein ASR ki wajah se unclear hain.

## 6. ERCRT, WHW aur science claims

Storytelling Science wala video **ERCRT** model batata hai: Emotion cycle, Relatability, Climax-at-start, Reason of watching, Twisting patterns. Is mein **WHW (What, How, Why)** bhi diya gaya hai. Source EDU-SRC0114. Yeh dono taught models hain; externally tested rules nahi.

Isi video mein neural coupling, oxytocin aur pareidolia ko audience reaction se link kiya gaya hai. Humein sirf itna pata chalta hai ke creator **yeh daaway karta hai**. Humne koi scientific research check nahi ki. Oxytocin ka measured result, 8-second human attention limit, ya guaranteed viral formula yahan se prove nahi hota.
