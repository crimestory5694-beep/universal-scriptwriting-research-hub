## Research findings and careful limits
- **CAT:** Context–Adversity–Takeaway; **CARD:** Context–Adversity–Resolution–Takeaway; **PAST:** Place–Action–Speech–Thoughts. These are creator-taught writing options, **not scientifically established retention laws**. A long factual documentary can omit an imposed takeaway or resolution when the evidence is incomplete.
- **Weather:** An unfolding forecast cannot be given a fabricated ending, storm impact or location to fit a story template. Current National Weather Service forecasts explicitly include uncertainty and model differences: https://www.weather.gov/about/forecast-process .
- **Military/history:** Third-party celebrity or corporate anecdotes, commanders' motives, private thoughts, and reconstructed direct speech require authenticated original sourcing. AP editorial standards require accurate quotations, attribution and no fabricated reporting: https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/ .
- **AI Skill tutorials:** Trigger examples, reference files, modular skills, test-correct-share and version-dependent tools belong to skill engineering; they are not evidence of increased YouTube audience retention. Restrict connector access, credentials and downloaded skill code; verify licenses and current product schemas.
- **Synthetic visuals:** YouTube's disclosure rule covers realistic AI-generated events or changed real-world footage; this is a publication requirement, not a script-performance effect: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en .
- **Duration specificity:** Creator advice from a 60-second social video must not be treated as an empirically validated pacing limit for a 15–25-minute documentary.
