## Findings — research-source claims versus independently established evidence

**Editorial fact boundary.** Original educator examples include imagined coffee-shop detail, unrecorded direct speech, film/television arcs, fictional sales calls, synthetic avatar demonstrations and attributed internal thoughts. They may be used as labeled teaching examples, not converted into authentic nonfiction dialogue, eyewitness reports or biographical events. Concrete detail in weather, military and history documentary narration needs direct documentary support. Associated Press editorial guidelines require trustworthy attribution and accurate quotations: https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/ .

**Writing candidates without claimed causal effects.** Stakes, truthful subject-specific openings, source-grounded behavior, open questions and explanatory closing summaries may be tried as creative approaches. Yet creator descriptions of 'brain-proven' narrative rules, guaranteed attention, audience loyalty, dopamine rewards, the Zeigarnik effect or universal memory gains are not original controlled YouTube viewing experiments. Retention causality remains UNTESTED, and an evolving weather forecast or conflict must not be given a fabricated resolution.

**Packaging/analytics do not equal scripting.** Title-thumbnail relationship, focal-point sequencing, CTR by traffic source, creator-persona models, channel distribution hypotheticals and sales campaign anecdotes are separate packaging/market/analytics topics. YouTube Studio's public A/B testing documentation explicitly uses watch-time outcomes, permits inconclusive results and does not endorse a fixed CTR uplift: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en . Creator accounts of recommendation seeding are not independently verified internal algorithm mechanics.

**AI/video/skill demonstrations.** Image/video generation prompts, celebrity-style footage, voice/avatar imitation, interactive budget charts, automatic responses, and tool/connector skill tutorials require rights, provenance, consent, permission scopes and live-version checks. They are production or implementation examples—not factual interview testimony or evidence of better writing engagement.
