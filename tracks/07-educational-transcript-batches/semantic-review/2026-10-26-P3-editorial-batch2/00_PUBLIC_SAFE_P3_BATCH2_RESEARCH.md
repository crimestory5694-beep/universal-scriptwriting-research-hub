## Substantive editorial decisions
- Source lessons about autobiographical suffering, emotional recall and crisis stakes do not justify pressuring interviewees for traumatic details, inventing military dialogue, narrating undocumented private thoughts, or creating false danger in live forecasts.
- Placing sourced action and geographic context early, concise concept explanations and an honest takeaway are optional *editorial experiments*, not fixed-second viewer-retention formulas. Do not force a resolved ending on an unfinished war, disaster or investigation.
- Creator anecdotes about growth, virality, subscriber/view mismatches, old recommendation models, evergreen controversy traffic or learning percentages lack the required cohorts, original studies and current platform verification. Route to research/analytics, not narrative fact.
- AI video pipelines, character-image prompts, clinic-like avatars, external AI web queries, scriptwriting skill tutorials and third-party API integrations are implementation and rights/security topics. Validate current capabilities, permissions, licensed likenesses, disclosures and secrets isolation; never recycle exposed tutorial API keys.
- Unconfirmed named vendor transcripts and second-hand course reviews must remain explicitly unverified, rather than being 'repaired' into a real site or credentialed product recommendation.
- Original AP editorial guidance rejects fabricated reporting and requires faithful attribution: https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/ . Official YouTube A/B testing is selected by watch-time outcomes and may be inconclusive, which does not establish a creator-taught scriptwriting formula: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en .
