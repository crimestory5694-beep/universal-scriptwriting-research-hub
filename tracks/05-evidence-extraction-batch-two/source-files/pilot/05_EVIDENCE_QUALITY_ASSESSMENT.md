## Acceptance scale

- **Q4** original human empirical study with identifiable methods, participants and outcomes, yet external transfer still open.
- **Q3** official institutional definitions/standards, strong for what issuer prescribes/defines, but **not empirical validation**.
- **Q2** attributed practitioner interviews or editorial cases; possible insights but unmeasured effectiveness.
- **Q1** partial text, indexed excerpt, or official metadata/press release; no comprehensive document-level conclusion.
- **Q0** blocked content; **no extraction**.

## Evidence vs validation

- **R163** study n=487 provides quantitative observed reading/listening comprehension and recall contrasts; design separates modalities but random allocation is not confirmed; no tested viewer retention/watch time. Efficacy transfer to written YouTube narration **unproved**.
- **R098** is correct primary definition for available YouTube Studio metrics; does not constitute public retention observations for selected videos.

- **R052 and R140 full guide** provide no source-grounded extracted writing practices this turn.

## Human study R163 minimum dataset

- Population: US Amazon Mechanical Turk crowdsourcing workers, primarily male and White; self-selected; screened for hearing ability.
- n=487 analyzed; 274 text and 213 synthesized audio. 716 source health text passages across 5 groups (193+40+40+243+200). Several HITs may be taken by participant; sample unit is not automatically 716 independent listeners.
- Audio: Azure default US male synthetic TTS with fixed default settings; no optimization of human intonation.
- Measures: perceived difficulty on 5-point scale; four comprehension questions (2 MC + 2 T/F; LLM generated, physician checked); free recall scored by exact-word overlap and embedding semantic similarity.
- Examples: Patient instructions 86% text, 66% audio; BMJ journal 76% text, 58% audio; WebMD 75% vs 55%; BMJ lay 72% vs 69%. Values are reported comprehension percentages; differences are 20/18/20/3 **percentage points**, not standardized effect sizes or universal expected gains.
- Explicit nulls: perceived difficulty WebMD/patient instructions difference nonsignificant; audio SMOG-comprehension r=-0.05 not significant; semantic free recall only BMJ journal significant between modality groups.
- Correlations: audio SMOG with two free-recall measures r=-0.12 and -0.14 (reported significant); no causality from correlations.
- Transfer exclusions: no YouTube content, thumbnail, audience-retention curve, natural actor VO, B-roll or multilingual validation.
