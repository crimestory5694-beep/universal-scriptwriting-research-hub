## Strongest findings with separate evidence semantics

1. **R163 (original academic):** healthcare reading vs default synthetic voice, n=487 (274 text vs 213 audio) and separate comprehension/free-recall measures. Text generally greater comprehension; correlations vary and some audio recall associations are significant. **Not evidence for YouTube audience retention**.
2. **R149 (W3C):** accessibility may require important image information to be included in audible explanation; conflicts with blanket redundancy removal. Standards-linked recommendations, **not causal tests of hook performance**.
3. **R098 (YouTube):** owner-facing retention curve indicators are descriptions; a peak may signify rewatch or confusion. **Not proof of 60–90-second re-hooks**.
4. **R137 (IDA):** nonfiction writing can be collaborative and nonlinear, with actual disagreements among professionals about writing/editing; **not one universal documentary script model**.
5. **R145 (RTDNA):** verified primary code establishes editorial ethics, not viewer engagement effects.
6. **R057 and R142:** two source-origin practitioner cases in natural spoken reporting and live sports radio/TV; neither is an A/B trial.
7. **R154:** actual first-party indexed TV documentary narration example, but not a draft script and not directly opened.
8. **R052/R140:** carefully quarantined missing actual source text; full content not claimed.

## Research conclusion

This pilot establishes **traceable constrained evidence for subject-specific writing hypotheses and counterexamples**, not sufficient population-level or causal data to generalize successful script construction. Empirical comprehension, audio recall and YouTube retention remain distinct variables.
