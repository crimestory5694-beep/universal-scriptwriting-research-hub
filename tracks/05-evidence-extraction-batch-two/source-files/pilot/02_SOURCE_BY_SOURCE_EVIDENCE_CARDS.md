## 07. R163 — Parallel Corpus Analysis of Text and Audio Comprehension

**Original URL:** https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e69772/
**Publisher / origin:** Journal of Medical Internet Research / original authors
**Access / actual depth:** READ_ORIGINAL_HTML · FULL_ARTICLE_SECTIONS_METHODS_RESULTS_LIMITATIONS_READ
**Type / date:** Peer-reviewed human-participant empirical study; 2025-10-02 journal publication
**Evidence location:** Ahmed et al., JMIR 2025 27:e69772; Methods 178–205; results 209–221; correlations Tables 2–5 (229–346); limitations 366–373; license 448–461
**Original analytical observations:** Different presentations of health texts were evaluated for perceived difficulty, multiple-choice/true-false comprehension, and free recall. Text outperformed default TTS audio on comprehension; readability formulas failed to predict comprehension in studied materials, while some had modest associations with free recall, including audio SMOG.
**Methods / study data / other evidence:** n=487 retained participants (text n=274, audio n=213); US Amazon Mechanical Turk, self-selection after hearing screen; parallel audio/text conditions; audio made with Azure TTS default US male voice/rate and no tuned pauses; stimulus document collections BMJ Lay n=193, WebMD n=40, patient instructions n=40, Simple Wikipedia n=243, BMJ journal n=200. Questions: 2 MC, 2 TF, recall, perceived Likert. BMJ journal MC/TF 76% text vs 58% audio (18 pp); patient instructions 86% vs 66% (20 pp); WebMD 75% vs 55% (20 pp); BMJ Lay 72% vs 69% (3 pp). Comprehension significant across corpora per authors. Perceived difficulty difference null for WebMD and patient instructions. Free-recall exact matching significant for patient instructions and BMJ; semantic recall only BMJ. Audio SMOG exact/similar recall r=-0.12/-0.14 significant; SMOG/audio comprehension r=-0.05 null. No standardized d or causal script effect reported in read sections.
**Category / niche / formats / audience:** Education → Spoken comprehension → Read-versus-heard difficulty, recall and readability formulas; format: Audio | Research study; audience: Health-information readers and listeners
**Applicability exceptions and limitations:** Most English-speaking-home sample; ~67% male, >94% White; TTS default voice not natural human VO; audio without video/visual cues; self-selected tasks; limited health domain, US crowd workers; question generation by GPT-4 expert-checked. Between-person allocation randomization not explicitly established; correlational text-feature analyses are not interventions. Recall retention is NOT YouTube watch retention.
**Independence family:** JMIR 2025 text-audio study
**Copyright / permitted use:** Open access Creative Commons Attribution 4.0; cite authors, article, journal and DOI. Third-party examples/supplements should still be screened. Related rights information: https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e69772/
