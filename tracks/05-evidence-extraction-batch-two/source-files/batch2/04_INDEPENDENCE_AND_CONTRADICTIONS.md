## Actual contrasts (not forced contradictions)

1. **Narrative integrity vs editorial compression:** R133 BBC 2012 can allow truth-preserving composite animal-life-cycle sequences when audience not misled; F-100's blanket composite character ban needs a nonfiction-domain exception. The source does *not* license fabricated testimony or characters in reported human history.
2. **Accessibility vs simplification:** R150 captions must include meaning-bearing sounds, and carefully timed speech; directly qualifies simplistic F-172 on-screen redundancy bans. R149 and R150 same institution; accessibility is not equivalent to automatic comprehension gains.
3. **Format-specific length:** R079 short edX chunks have higher normalized viewing and higher problem-attempt association, but tutorials are often replayed and the authors find different patterns from lectures. This does not authorize the F-180 uniform 8–12-minute structure; it also cannot justify 6-minute limits for general-interest documentaries.
4. **Measured null:** R079 PH207x postvideo problem-attempt rate 33% with/without talking-head style, unlike 6.00x 46% vs 33%. Do not conceal this when discussing talking-head associations.
5. **Sports timing vs hooks:** R141 says scoreboard orientation about once per 90 sec in live radio, and an 8–10-minute sport recap. Neither is evidence for F-181 narrative retention-loop timing.
6. **Shorts raw starts vs meaningful watches:** R160 metrics separate in-feed exposure and viewed-vs-swiped. R161 2026 policy counts playback starts across formats but YPP uses distinct engaged/qualified metrics. Raw view counts cannot establish script-caused retention nor cleanly support cross-era virality rankings.
7. **SEC vs CFPB:** Although similar novice financial topics, R156/R157 focus investment risk and portfolio concepts, R158/R159 focus consumer finances and educator use. No study compares these different organizations' approaches experimentally.
