## Card 01 — R079: How Video Production Affects Student Engagement: An Empirical Study of MOOC Videos
- **Original registered URL:** https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf
- **Publisher:** Philip J. Guo / Juho Kim / Rob Rubin; ACM Learning@Scale / MIT CSAIL-hosted copy
- **Original publication/access:** 2014 ACM Learning@Scale, DOI 10.1145/2556325.2566239; ORIGINAL_PDF_READ
- **Evidence class and depth:** Peer-reviewed observational mixed-methods research; retrospective logs; SUBSTANTIVE_ACADEMIC_PAPER_TEXT_AND_PAGES_1_5_8_VISUALLY_CHECKED
- **Independence family:** `Guo_Kim_Rubin_edX_2014`; sources sharing a publisher are not independent replications.
- **Taxonomy:** Education → Online learning → MOOC instructional video production and engagement proxies. **Independent format:** Lecture|Tutorial|Instructional video. **Audience:** 2012 edX learners; math/science MOOCs. **Language:** English.
- **Exact primary citation locator:** https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf — PDF pp.1–8, table 2, figures 2–8, methodology and limitations
- **Source-grounded observations:** The authors analyze distinct online instruction delivery modes and find viewing-session duration and post-video assessment-attempt differences varying with production style and length. Observed 0–3 min videos have high normalized watching, while tutorial rewatch patterns differ from lectures. Interspersing talking head improved watch time in two courses, but problem-attempt gains occurred in 6.00x (46% vs 33%) and not PH207x (33% vs 33%). Speaking rate correlates with engagement, but instructors’ enthusiasm, lecture planning and medium covary.
- **Methods/data/controls:** Retrospective edX logs from 4 fall-2012 math/science courses: 862 videos, 127,839 students reported across four course rows (unique individuals across courses not established), 6,902,358 viewing sessions; qualitative interviews with 6 production staff. Two proxies: watching-session duration and post-video problem attempt within 30min. Videos grouped by length and production style; reports nonparametric 95% median CI error bars, Mann-Whitney/chi-square p<.001 in selected comparisons, no randomized script intervention or comprehensive numerical CI table. 1x playback-only figures ~76% sessions. Session starts <5s removed for autoplay.
- **Exceptions, nulls and counterarguments:** No random assignment and no script-text isolation; four math/science MOOCs, self-selected educational viewers, observational confounding. Watch time is neither attention nor comprehension; attempting an assessment is neither passing it nor later remembering. Intro speaking pace effects not proven causal. Maximum watched ~6 min in this sample cannot mandate <6 minute YouTube video duration; lecture/tut distinction.
- **Copyright/access:** © authors/ACM; PDF grants personal/classroom copying with credit and conditions, not general commercial republishing; DOI and research summaries only.. Rights reference: https://doi.org/10.1145/2556325.2566239
- **Possible F-IDs (not approved):** F-063, F-170, F-180, F-022
- **Assessment:** Q4 LARGE_OBSERVATIONAL_EMPIRICAL_NARROW_SCOPE; STRONG_CANDIDATE_FOR_CONTEXT_SPECIFIC_REPLICATION_NOT_CAUSAL_SCRIPT_RULE.

## Specific measured results — R079 only

Guo/Kim/Rubin (2014): 862 distinct videos, 127,839 students reported across four course rows (cross-course de-duplication not specified), 6,902,358 sessions, 4 edX math/science courses in Fall 2012; interviews with 6 production staff. The unit for 6.9M is **viewing sessions**, NOT participants. Table 2, p.3.
Study: retrospective usage logs with statistical comparisons (not randomized script experiments). Session-length and problem-attempt proxies; <5-second autoplay-start sessions discarded. 1x speed roughly 76% of sessions used in plotted results. No comprehensibility or content recall metric.
Selected observed values: shortest 0–3-minute category highest normalized viewing; 75% of that shortest category lasted more than three quarters of its length; five ordered length buckets have subsequent problem-attempt rates 56%, 48%, 43%, 41%, 31% (Figure 2 discussion).
Talking head versus slides/code: 6.00x problem-attempt rate 46% vs 33%, PH207x 33% vs 33% (null comparison). Pauses/rewinds were not significantly different for talking-head vs comparison groups. Some nonparametric median 95% confidence intervals shown as figure error bars, but source does not provide universally transferable numerical confidence intervals.
Different-course preproduction contrast: 55% vs 41% problem attempts, but instructors and subjects differ. Faster delivery correlated with some session outcomes; reported observed speaking-rate range 48–254 wpm, mean 156, SD 31; not a voiceover speech-speed prescription. Lecture and tutorial usage diverged—tutorials more often revisited.
The authors explicitly warn that this is not controlled experimentation, that viewer attention is not observable from server events, and that their 4 math/science MOOC courses may not generalize.
